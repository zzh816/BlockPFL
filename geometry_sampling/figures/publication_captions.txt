mechanism_publication
=====================
Current-gradient mechanism experiments with 20 paired runs per configuration. Panels (a-c) show the mean pre-update time average of the squared full deterministic proximal-gradient mapping versus training client-block gradient calls; bands are nominal pointwise 95% Student-t confidence intervals for the run mean (19 degrees of freedom), not simultaneous bands, run SD, or theoretical gradient-estimator variance. Panel (a) starts with a large common gradient and small disagreement; panel (b) starts with a zero common gradient and larger disagreement, retaining the reverse comparison. Each method's mapping is evaluated at its own evolving iterates. Panel (c) uses the original nonconvex data with M=2. Panels (d,e) show run probabilities of any shared-block split and final heterogeneous-block fusion under the threshold sweep; bars are Wilson 95% intervals from 20 independent run indicators. The vertical dotted line marks the sufficient shared-block threshold tau=0.14 and has no fusion interpretation. Panel (f) shows the pre-update shared-block split time fraction at observation SD 0.4, M*T=2400, and 32 samples per client; bars are pointwise Student-t intervals of the 20 run fractions, not intervals treating correlated updates as independent trials. Panels (a-e) vary algorithmic sampling on fixed data; panel (f) varies both training data and independent algorithmic sampling. Numerical split/fusion tolerance is 1e-10. Paired schedules and data are shared between methods; displayed intervals are for individual method means. Diagnostic full gradients and preprocessing are excluded from the training-call axis and are accounted for separately in the archived protocol.

baseline_publication
====================
Additional method comparison on 32-client, four-block nonconvex problems with block dimension eight, M=8, and 20 paired runs per case. All methods receive a total budget of 8000 training client-block gradient calls, including the gradient-table method's 128-call initialization; diagnostic full gradients are excluded and separately accounted for. Panels (a,b) report the budget-weighted running mean of the squared full deterministic proximal-gradient mapping: initialization calls retain the initial-state mapping, and each selected-block update is weighted by its training gradient calls. Thus the same call axis and initial-cost accounting apply to all methods. The methods are Geometric, Inverse inclusion, and the implemented client gradient-table baseline; the latter is a gradient-memory reference method. Panel (c) shows each method's final empirical objective, to be compared within each case. Lines/points are run means; bands/bars are nominal pointwise 95% Student-t confidence intervals with 19 degrees of freedom. They are not run SD or simultaneous bands; intervals describe individual method means rather than paired method differences. Data and algorithmic schedules remain paired across methods. Both cases are synthetic, use the same fixed selected-block stepsize 0.015 across methods, and include no per-method tuning. The coupled case is a stress experiment, and generated zero-tilt labels are not claims about the coupled population optimum.
